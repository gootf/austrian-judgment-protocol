# Cheatsheet — Austrian Judgment Protocol

*Decision rules. Claim types marked per rule. Source: [F&K] = Foss & Klein; [Packard] = Packard.*

## Part 1 — Demand-side triage [Packard]

### Opportunity triage (Ch2 p27-28; Ch5 p122-123 — source-derived)
| IF | THEN | BECAUSE |
|---|---|---|
| No recognized, imperfectly satisfied consumer need | Do NOT treat as an opportunity — return to discovery | Needs are latent; demand must be recognized by consumers, not assumed (p13) |
| Team has technical knowledge but weak needs knowledge | Acquire needs knowledge (user research, lead users) before building | One-sided knowledge → Einstellung entrenchment, not innovation (p45) |
| Team has needs knowledge but no technical/affordance knowledge | Search for resource affordances (uncertain search) or partner | Innovation requires both knowledge types (p45) |
| Value-to-cost judgment not yet made | Do NOT commit capital — judgment is a necessary third stage | Entrepreneurship cannot occur without the capitalist's judgment (p34) |
| Entrepreneur strongly confident in demand | Discount the estimate — systematic overestimation of need-sharing | Entrepreneurs overestimate how similar others are (p35-36) |

### Capitalist's three judgments (Ch2 p35-36 — source-derived)
1. **Cost/viability**: total expected cost incl. opportunity cost vs. willingness to bear risk (wealth buffer raises tolerance).
2. **Economic value**: expected consumer demand/benefit — correct for overconfidence bias.
3. **Actor ability**: perception of the entrepreneur's capability to execute.

### Innovation mechanics (Ch3 p45, p49-59 — source-derived)
- Innovation = needs knowledge × technical knowledge; both necessary.
- Affordance-first order is common (know resources → perceive need); need-first requires active uncertain search.
- Only accessibility (recent recall) was supported as a moderator — treat personality/engagement claims skeptically.
- Scientific discovery → affordance spikes → waves of (often radical) innovation.

### Diffusion & performance (Ch4; Ch5 p123 — source-derived)
- Consumer uncertainty (not just product quality) drives: S-curve adoption, failures, chasm, first-mover advantage, continuous innovation.
- First mover sustains advantage when: product value is large (fast uncertainty reduction), imitators are weak brands, or imitators are too slow (legitimacy captured).
- Continuous innovation extends product life beyond market saturation.

### Framework positioning (Ch2 p14, p39-41; Ch5 p122-124 — source-derived)
- Discovery (Kirzner/Shane), creation (Alvarez), judgment (Foss & Klein) = stages of ONE process, not rival theories.
- Purpose of entrepreneurship = consumer well-being, not profit; demand-pull (Kirzner 1999).
- Supply-side assumptions to reject: opportunity pre-existence, predictability, profit motivation, entrepreneur-as-growth-source.

### Agent-derived operating rules [Packard] (NOT in Packard's text — marked)
- Before funding: run the 3-condition conjunction check; return proposals to the failed condition instead of kill/fund wholesale. [agent-derived]
- Prefer uncertainty-reduction experiments (need validation, market tests) over forecasts when committing resources. [agent-derived]
- For tech-first teams: treat missing needs knowledge as the primary risk, not a secondary concern. [agent-derived]

## Part 2 — Judgment authority [F&K]

### Delegation triage (source-derived: p192-196, p219)
| IF | THEN | BECAUSE |
|---|---|---|
| Decision is business-idea formation/execution | Retain as original judgment (owner level) | Original judgment is the entrepreneur-owner's (p192) |
| Decision is execution within a plan | Delegate as derived judgment, with constraints + monitoring | Derived judgment is delegated, monitored, means-to-an-end (p219) |
| Complete contract would remove all discretion | Do NOT write it — leave incompleteness | Complete contracts curb productive entrepreneurship (p219-220) |
| Destructive proxy entrepreneurship dominates (rent-seeking/influence) | Tighten organizational structure, not just contract | Structure balances productive vs destructive (p192) |
| Authority holder lacks capacity-judgment | Reassign — hierarchy requires judging capacity, not problem details | Knight 1921:296 (p193-194) |

### Authority & knowledge (source-derived: p213-218, p219)
- Dispersed knowledge (Hayek 1945) challenges hierarchy — respond with **authority backed by ownership**, not abolition of hierarchy.
- Middle managers = information filters + legitimacy signals (resource allocation).
- Even knowledge-based firms need centralized coordination — top management reserves ultimate decision rights.

### Agent-system mapping (agent-derived)
- CEO = original judgment holder; CTO/team = derived judgment (delegated, monitored).
- Delegation to sub-agents: constrained empowerment + reserved ultimate rights.
- Information filters are a feature, not a bug: compress upward, signal downward.

### Cross-source rules (cross-source-derived — NOT in any single source)
- "Do not centralize all knowledge in the CEO agent" = Hayek (dispersed knowledge) + F&K (authority) + Packard A005 (uncertainty experiments).
- Refactor vs feature-velocity conflicts = F&K (who holds judgment) + Rumelt (strategic kernel) + Hayek (knowledge distribution) — decide by judgment-classification, not by preference.
