**Authors**: Nicolai J. Foss & Peter G. Klein | **Format**: Cambridge UP monograph | **Compiled**: ch08 (core chapter) | **Claims**: claims.yaml (25: incl. capability-boundary dimension, Christensen)

## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. Original vs Derived Judgment (ch08, p192-193)
Original judgment = entrepreneurial formation/execution of a business idea (owner). Derived judgment = employee judgment within the entrepreneur's constraints. Employees exercising derived judgment are **proxy entrepreneurs**. Use when: classifying who should decide.

### 2. Productive vs Destructive Proxy Entrepreneurship (p192)
Proxy entrepreneurship can increase or reduce firm value. Contracts + organizational design exist to balance the two. Use when: designing discretion for teams/agents.

### 3. Knightian Hierarchy (p193-194)
Managers judge subordinates' *capacity* to deal with problems, not concrete knowledge of the problems. Use when: deciding what a superior can meaningfully evaluate.

### 4. Contract Incompleteness as Feature (p219-220)
Complete contracts curb entrepreneurial activity — incompleteness is not a cost failure. Organizational structure, not formal contracts, limits discretion under uncertainty. Use when: designing contracts or agent agreements.

### 5. Authority Backed by Ownership + Centralized Coordination (p213-219)
Dispersed knowledge challenges hierarchy, but the answer is authority backed by ownership — even knowledge-based firms need centralized coordination; top management reserves ultimate decision rights. Use when: configuring decision rights in orgs or agent architectures.

### 6. Capability-Boundary Dimension (Christensen)
What an organization can *actually execute* ≠ what is technically possible — capability is determined jointly by the value network (customer/investor structure), resource-allocation processes, and organization size (CH07). This is a judgment dimension of authority-assignment: it asks "What can our organization actually execute?" — independent of both "what is the challenge?" (Rumelt R01) and "who owns judgment?" (FK02). Runtime rules (CH02/CH05/CH06/CH08):
1. **Resource-dependence check** — resource allocation is decided by customers and investors, not managers; ask "who is funding what" (CH02)
2. **No quantification precondition in disruptive scenarios** — demanding quantified markets and financial returns before investing paralyzes disruptive moves; use small experiments instead (CH05)
3. **Organization-scale matching** — a large organization cannot focus adequate energy on small markets; hand disruptive commercialization to an independent unit matched to the target market (CH06)
4. **Value-network blind-spot check** — decisions rational inside the current value network are blind spots outside it; entering a new market requires a new value framework (CH08)

Both directions of failure have anchors: a CTO vetoing business exploration on "architectural elegance" oversteps derived judgment (FK05); a CEO demanding quantification before funding misapplies sustaining-planning methods to disruptive moves (CH09).

Use when: deciding whether the organization can actually execute a chosen strategy — before committing resources to it.
