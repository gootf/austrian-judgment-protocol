## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The Three-Stage Entrepreneurial Process (ch02, p14, p27-34)
Entrepreneurship = "the process by which unmet consumer needs are newly addressed" — NOT exploitation of market imperfections. Three stages, all required:
1. **Discovery/Recognition** (consumer) — unmet needs are latent, discovered by consumers through experience
2. **Solution Generation** (innovator) — creative combination of needs knowledge × technical/affordance knowledge
3. **Judgment/Action** (capitalist) — total economic value vs. cost/effort, under producer uncertainty; the resource owner bears ultimate risk

Use it when: evaluating whether an opportunity is complete; the answer is a conjunction, not a single factor.

### 2. Consumer Sovereignty (ch02, p13-14)
Consumers direct production; the entrepreneur is bound to obey ("the captain is the consumer" — Mises 1949:270). Use it when: deciding who defines value — always the consumer, never the producer's assumption.

### 3. The Knowledge-Intersection Model of Innovation (ch03, p45, p49-59)
Innovation = needs knowledge × technical knowledge. One without the other → **Einstellung effect** (entrenchment; experts high in one type only are *less* innovative). Only accessibility supported as moderator. Use it when: diagnosing why innovation stalls in a technically strong team.

### 4. The Three Judgments of the Capitalist (ch02, p35-36)
Cost/viability → economic value (bias: entrepreneurs overestimate need-sharing → overconfidence) → actor ability. Use it when: preparing to commit capital; build in disconfirmation.

### 5. Unification of Entrepreneurship Theories (ch02 p39-41; ch05 p122-124)
Discovery (Kirzner/Shane), creation (Alvarez), judgment (Foss & Klein) are **different aspects of one process** — conjointly realized: needs discovered by consumer, solutions created by innovator, outcomes imagined/judged by capitalist. Use it when: reconciling competing frameworks instead of picking one.

### 6. Consumer Uncertainty as Performance Arbiter (ch04; ch05 p123)
Diffusion outcomes (S-curve, failure, chasm, first-mover advantage, continuous innovation) follow from consumer uncertainty resolution, not product quality alone. Use it when: forecasting adoption or explaining failure.
