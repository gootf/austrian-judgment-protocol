# Discovery Alertness — Opportunity Discovery Judgment Protocol

**Sources**: Kirzner *Competition and Entrepreneurship* | **Claims**: claims.yaml (9: 5 source-derived + 3 cross-source + 1 agent)

## How to Use
- **Without arguments**: load this framework (4 procedures) as the baseline for opportunity judgment
- **With a scenario**: market signal / new business / competitive change → run the 4 procedures (search → weak signals → equilibration → validation)
- **With a question**: `alertness` / `discovery profit` / `market process` / `secrets` → read the corresponding claims + coordinates
- **Cross**: demand-side (Packard) to validate demand reality; diagnosis (Rumelt) to diagnose the challenge

## Core Thesis
**Opportunities are discovered, not created**: alertness is the defining characteristic of entrepreneurship — the market process corrects errors through alertness and moves toward equilibrium; profit is the return to discovering unnoticed opportunities.

## Key Concepts
- **Alertness** (K01): alertness = defining feature of entrepreneurship (not plan execution)
- **Market process** (K02): changes in prices/output/methods are generated by the interaction of participants' decisions
- **Equilibration** (K03): discovering errors → correcting → tending toward equilibrium (direction of the process, not a premise)
- **Discovery profit** (K04): profit = return to unnoticed opportunities
- **Competition as discovery process** (K07): competition is the mechanism by which entrepreneurs discover consumer knowledge
- **Secrets** (KX01): Thiel — "important but undiscovered facts" = variant phrasing of alertness

## Decision Procedures (4, executable)
1. **Opportunity-search mode** (K01/K08): ask "what is unnoticed?" rather than "what is the plan?" — CEO search is alertness-based
2. **Weak-signal identification** (K04/KX03): growth/sales/customer-behavior changes → check weak signals first (opportunity/threat) rather than attributing to the market
3. **Equilibration correction** (K03): identify mispricing/misallocation → corrective action (before others)
4. **Demand-side cross-validation** (KX02): discovered "opportunities" must pass consumer-demand validation (Packard's three stages) — otherwise it is an illusion

## Anti-patterns
1. Treating "plan execution" as entrepreneurship (opportunity search without alertness)
2. Ignoring weak signals (attributing sales decline directly to the market)
3. Filtering new opportunities through an existing framework (value-network blind spot)
4. Treating "novel concepts" as "discoveries" (without demand validation — KX02 anti-pattern)
