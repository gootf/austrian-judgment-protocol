# Glossary — Austrian Judgment Protocol
*Terms alphabetized. Source noted per term: [F&K] = Foss & Klein, Organizing Entrepreneurial Judgment; [Packard] = Packard; [Kirzner] = Competition and Entrepreneurship; [Mises] = Human Action; [Taleb] = The Black Swan & Antifragile; [Hayek] = The Use of Knowledge in Society; [E&S] = Ekelund & Saurman, Advertising and the Market Process; [framework] = this framework's own terms. Provenance: (section, page).*
- **Affordance** — a use to which a resource may be employed under the proper circumstances; knowledge of affordances derives from technical knowledge and expertise (Gibson 1966; Penrose 1959). [Packard] (Ch2, p29-30)
- **Alertness** — the propensity to notice unnoticed opportunities; the defining characteristic of entrepreneurship — opportunity discovery precedes plan execution. [Kirzner] (Competition and Entrepreneurship)
- **Authority backed by ownership** — decision rights whose ultimate basis is ownership; serves to maximize joint surplus. [F&K] (Dispersed knowledge/authority, p213)
- **Barbell strategy** — extreme conservatism + extreme aggressiveness, rejecting the middle — the conversion mechanism from fragile to antifragile. [Taleb] (Antifragile)
- **Case probability** — probability of a unique event; not computable by frequency statistics; the domain of entrepreneurial judgment (vs class probability). [Mises] (Human Action ch.VI)
- **Central impossibility** — no single mind can possess all dispersed knowledge; the data of the economic calculus are never given to one mind. [framework: Hayek] (The Use of Knowledge in Society)
- **Class probability** — probability computable from frequency statistics over a class of events; the domain of natural science (vs case probability). [Mises] (Human Action ch.VI)
- **Consumer involvement** — deep passion and interest in a specific product or activity; the strongest predictor of who will need, search for, and create solutions (Havitz & Mannell 2005). [Packard] (Ch3, p49-50)
- **Consumer sovereignty** — the principle (Hutt 1936; Mises 1949) that consumers direct economic production; individuals hold dual producer/consumer roles; "the captain is the consumer." [Packard] (Ch2, p13-14)
- **Consumer uncertainty** — consumers' inherent uncertainty about the value of newly introduced solutions; its resolution over time drives diffusion patterns (S-curve, failures, chasm). [Packard] (Ch4, p77; Ch5, p123)
- **Contract incompleteness (Austrian rationale)** — contracts are incomplete not because completeness is costly, but because complete contracts curb entrepreneurial activity, both productive and destructive. [F&K] (Conclusions, p219-220)
- **Demand-pull entrepreneurship** — entrepreneurship as universally and inherently driven by consumer demand (Kirzner 1999), facilitated by dissemination of new technical/affordance knowledge. [Packard] (Ch5, p123)
- **Derived judgment** — judgment exercised by employees within limits set by the entrepreneur's plan; derived from the original judgment of the entrepreneur-owner. [F&K] (Original and derived judgment, p192)
- **Derived/delegated decision rights** — decision rights over resources given to non-owners are merely derived or delegated; ultimate rights remain with the owner (Foss, Foss & Klein 2007). [Packard] (Ch2, p33-34)
- **Dispersed knowledge** — knowledge not possessed by any single mind; may be private, fleeting, tacit, and subjectively held (O'Driscoll & Rizzo 1985); Hayek's (1945) challenge to authority and hierarchy. [F&K] (Dispersed knowledge/authority, p213)
- **Einstellung effect** — cognitive entrenchment: innovativeness decreases for experts high in only one type of knowledge (Dane 2010). [Packard] (Ch3, p45; Ch5, p123)
- **Imperfectly satisfied consumer needs** — the revised unit of opportunity analysis, replacing "corrigible market imperfections." [Packard] (Ch2, p13; Ch5, p123)
- **Information anti-filtering** — framework rule: reporting chains produce filtered, lagging, second-hand knowledge; central decisions must sample the raw periphery rather than rest on aggregates alone. [framework] (HX01)
- **Innovation** — the creative combination of knowledge of what can be done with knowledge of what needs to be done (Schumpeter 1934; Runco 1994); a problem-solving process at the intersection of needs knowledge and technical knowledge. [Packard] (Ch3, p45-46)
- **Judgment (capitalist)** — the third entrepreneurial stage: judging whether an innovation has enough total economic value relative to cost and effort to warrant market action, under producer uncertainty. [Packard] (Ch2, p33-35)
- **Knightian hierarchy** — hierarchy in which managers judge subordinates' *capacity* to deal with problems, not concrete knowledge of the problems ("knowledge of a man's capacity to deal with a problem, not concrete knowledge of the problem itself" — Knight 1921:296). [F&K] (Original and derived judgment, p193-194)
- **Knowledge-based approach** — greater knowledge facilitates novel combinations; personality/context matter only as facilitators of knowledge acquisition, recall, and combination (Weisberg 1999, 2006). [Packard] (Ch3, p49)
- **Lead users** — high-use consumers holding the highest needs and thus the most potential value from innovation (von Hippel 1986). [Packard] (Ch3, p50)
- **Local judgment allocation** — framework rule: decisions depending on time-and-place knowledge belong to the person closest to the facts, with central authority limited to coordination constraints. [framework] (H05)
- **Man on the spot** — the person closest to the particular circumstances of time and place; the ultimate decisions must be left to him, coordinated by signals. [Hayek] (The Use of Knowledge in Society)
- **Middle managers as information filters** — middle managers filter information between employees and the entrepreneur and signal project legitimacy via resource allocation. [F&K] (Dispersed knowledge/authority, p213)
- **Needs knowledge** — knowledge of consumers' needs (the problem side); tacit, personal, learned through experience (Witt 2001). [Packard] (Ch2, p13; Ch3, p45)
- **Open-ended universe** — a world in which decisionmakers may be ignorant of their own ignorance and subject to genuine surprise; advertising is attention-grabbing that opens consumers' eyes to unnoticed opportunities, not preference manipulation. [E&S, Kirzner foreword]
- **Original judgment** — the entrepreneurial formation and execution of a business idea; held by the entrepreneur-owner (or jointly by an ownership group). [F&K] (Original and derived judgment, p192-193)
- **Producer uncertainty** — entrepreneurs' inability to assure at judgment time that the solution will be accepted by consumers; "the hallmark of entrepreneurship" (Knight 1921). [Packard] (Ch2, p34)
- **Proxy entrepreneurship** — employees exercising derived judgment; the entrepreneur "makes his employees entrepreneurs themselves" (Knight 1921:276); productive (value-increasing) or destructive (value-reducing, rent-seeking, influence activities). [F&K] (ch08 opening; Original and derived judgment, p192-194)
- **Reversibility assessment** — framework rule: irreversible actions require the highest evidence bar; stage commitments and predefine kill criteria. [framework] (UX01)
- **Search/experience/credence goods** — goods classified by how consumers can evaluate quality: search (inspectable before purchase), experience (evaluable only after purchase), credence (not reliably evaluable even after); the informational role of advertising and consumer uncertainty differ by type. [E&S] (ch4)
- **Solution generation** — the second entrepreneurial stage: creative construction of solutions by applying useful knowledge to a recognized need (innovator's role). [Packard] (Ch2, p27-29)
- **Technical knowledge** — knowledge of resources, technologies, and their capabilities (the solution side). [Packard] (Ch3, p45)
- **Three-stage entrepreneurial process** — discovery/recognition of unmet needs (consumer) → solution generation (innovator) → judgment/action (capitalist); all three must complete for venturing to occur. [Packard] (Ch2, p14, p27-28)
- **Time-and-place knowledge** — non-scientific but important knowledge of particular circumstances; by nature cannot enter statistics or be conveyed centrally. [Hayek] (The Use of Knowledge in Society)
- **Triad** — fragile / robust / antifragile: systems harmed by, unaffected by, or benefiting from volatility. [Taleb] (Antifragile)
- **Unmet consumer needs** — latently existing needs discovered over time through experience; individually and objectively justifiable, replacing socially constructed demand. [Packard] (Ch2, p13)
- **Well-being (purpose of entrepreneurship)** — the purpose of entrepreneurship is consumer well-being, not profit (Hutt 1936; Mises 1949). [Packard] (Ch5, p124)
